Visual Numbers

To use numbers with visual mnemonics they must invoke mental visual images just like other objects.The problem is that numbers are intangible pieces of information. You can not visualize a number, when written it is just a symbol. Fortunately there is a memory system technique called the Major System to convert numbers to substitute mental visual images. Learning the Major System is more involved and requires more rote memorization than what has been discussed previously but still uses the basic building blocks of mental visual images and associations.

In addition to the Major System there are several other visual systems for remembering numbers that are simplier though not near as powerful that will be discussed briefly at the end of this page.

The Major System is the most powerful and useful memory system technique for numbers. It is recommended if you are interested in using memory system techniques in your everyday life. See More Uses for additional way a quick strong memoey of numbers can be beneficial.

The Major System

The Major System once grasped can revolutionize ones ability to recall numbers. Remembering an 8 digit number with rote memorization is time consuming and often inaccurate but with the Major System it becomes easy!

The Major System works by a series of mental associations/conversions:

  • Individual digits are associated with phonetic sounds (letters).
  • Phonetic sounds/letters are then converted to words (mental visual images).

(Lorayne and Lucas chapter 10)

As discussed earlier in this web site most users of memory system techniques sometimes personalize a basic concept ina way that works better for them. The "Major System" is the area that I personalized more than other areas and where I have done so I will mark with a ★ and explain why

Associate Digits With Phonetic Sounds (Letters)

There are 10 digits that make up all numbers. In the Major System 10 basic consonant phonetic sounds are associated with each of the 10 digits. Phonetic sounds for the purpose of these pages means certain sounds we produce by the same configurations of our vocal tract when pronouncing letters, T and D are an example of having same phonetic sound (Lorayne and Lucas chapters 10 & 11). This does require the use of rote memorization but in the long run it makes memorizing numbers vastly easier.

The following are the digits and associated phonectic sounds and consonants of the Major System. After reviewing this list I will discuss below why some letters are red.

  • 0 = s, z, soft c (e.g. century, ceiling) all of which have the same hissing phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint zero starts with z.
  • 1 = t or d phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint is a small t has a single down stroke.
  • 2 = n phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint is a n small two down strokes.
  • 3 = m phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint is a small m has three down strokes.
  • 4 = r phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint is the word four ends with an r or a 4 looks somewhat like backwards small case r .
  • 5 = l phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint is capital L looks like a 5 finger hand with the thumb sticking out.
  • 6 = sh, ch, j (e.g. jude), soft g (e.g. gentle) phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint 6 looks like a left shoe footprint.
  • 7 = k, hard c (e.g. cat, cut), ck (e.g. Duck), hard g (e.g. gut) phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint is capital K looks like it could be made up of two 7's back to back.
  • 8 = f, v, ph phonectic sound. Helpful memory hint is a cursive small f looks like an 8.
  • 9 = p, b phonetic sound. Helpful memory hint is a 9 looks like backwards capital P.

Additional Major System rules:

  • Vowels (a,e,i,o,u) have no meaning.
  • Consonants w, h and y have no meaning.
  • Consonant x is usually not used.
  • Silient consonants have no meaning. Gnash would be 26 (use the n and sh) as the g is silient
  • Double letters count as single letter as usually the make one phonetic sound, Moss would be 30 (use the m and use s) and not 300.

★ In the Major System classically the number is linked to the phonectic sound and not the letter(s). When I began learning the Major System I sometimes had a difficulty with some of the phonectic sounds and it was not second nature to me so I adapted what I called my "My Modified Major System". Instead of learning all of the letters associated with each phonectic sound I just learned one letter with each digit, these are listed in red above. This initially made learning the major System easier for me but limited me some as discussed below in the next segment. I was able to make the numbers 0-100 with this modification that are listed on the page More Numbers but with numbers past 100 it was just not possible to come up with the appropriate word. Fortunately ss I used the system more I got more comfortable with phonetic sounds and eventually was able to make words for numbers above 100 using all the letters associated with a phonetic sound.

Convert Phonetic Sounds(Letters) into Words (Mental visual Images)

After converting the 10 digits into phonetic sounds (letters) whole words can be made by adding vowels and other letters in line with the Major System rules outlined above. The resulting words can then be visualized as mental visual images that represent the original numbers. Once we get the mental visual image of a number then associations, both links and the method of loci, can be used with the mental visual image as before.

This makes the Major System very powerful memory system as it allows for the creation a numerous number of words and mental visual images to represent numbers.

★As discussed above when I started with the Major System I limited the letters that represented each digit to those in red above and premade numbers 0-100 Even then limiting the letters representing each digit to a single letter using phonics helped me recall a number such as 10 listed below. Make the phonectic sound for the t and then the s and usually the word you using, e.g. toes, will pop into your mind.

The following are examples of the first eleven numbers and the resulting mental visual images I personally use. Also I gave an example how each mental visual image could be linked with the previous or next mental visual image in a list.

  • 0 = saw. Remember vowels and w have no meaning. Picture a handsaw cutting the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 1 = tie. Remember vowels have no meaning. Mentally visualize an image of a bright red tie. For a link the tie could be wrapped around the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 2 = Noah. Remember vowels and h have no meaning. Mentally visualize an image of an elderly man dressed in a white robe with a beard and a staff. For a link Noah could be standing on the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 3 = mow. Remember vowels and w have no meaning. Mentally visualize an image of a lawn mower. For link it could be mowing the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 4 = row. Remember vowels and w have no meaning. Mentally visualize an image of an oar. For a link the previous or next mental visual image. could be doing the rowing.
  • 5 = Law. Remember vowels and w have no meaning. Mentally visualize an image of a gold lawman's badge in the shape of a star with sharp points. For a link it could be imbedded with the sharp points in the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 6 = shoe. Remember vowels and h have no meaning. Picture an old brown shoe. For a link the previous or next mental visual image could be in the shoe.
  • 7 = key. Remember vowels and y have no meaning. Picture an antique gold key. For a link in can be sticking in the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 8 = fee. Remember vowels have no meaning. I picture a stack of gold coins. For a link they could be covering up and crushing the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 9 = paw. Picture a severed animals paw (remember a gruesome mental image is stronger) or you could use pie. Remember vowels and w have no meaning. For a link picture the paw could be pawing the previous or next mental visual image.
  • 10 = toes. Remember vowels have no meaning. Picture toes walking on the previous or next mental visual image.

The Peg System

Before we go on it would be good to discuss one of the benefits of using "premade" mental visual images vs "made on the fly" mental visual images of numbers. First as discssed above unless you are really proficient in using the Major Memory System it is quicker if you already have a constant list of mental visual image for at least some numbers. Second if you have "premade mental visual images" you find what is called the Peg System useful. The following paragraphs will explain by example how the Peg Systemworks and demonstate how it may be useful.

You may already know by "rote" memory numbers 1-10 on the preceding page, if not take a moment to learn them. AI says the 5 most common causes of death in the United States in last year were heart disease, cancer, unintentional injuries, stroke and chronic lung disease. I can remeber these by the link method where a would see a beating heart(heart disease) with a crab(cancer) eating it with its claws. I hit the crab with a hammer (unintentional injuries) to get it off the heart and the crab shoots out lightening bolts (stroke) when it is struck and cracks open. The lightening bolts pop a balloon shpaed as lungs(chronic lung disease) nearby.

Now you could even rememeber a much longer list by linking mental images but if someone asked you for the 4th most common cause of death you would probably how to recall them one by one sequentially. The Peg System makes this easier to go straight to the 4th or even 40th item in a list. To do this lets link the causes of death to the numbers we have learned. I picture a tie wrapping aound a beating heart, Noah with a crab implaed by his staff, a lawn mowing running over a pile of hammers with a terrible noise, a man rowing a boat getting struck with lightening bolts, and a law badge popping a lung shaped ballon with one of it's sharp points. Now if someone where to ask me the 4th most common cause I would picture a man rowing a boat getting strick lightening bolts and know it was strokes.

Other Visual Number Systems

There are other memory systems that can be used to memorize numbers such as the Shaper System and the Rhyme System.

The Shaper System assigns a mental visual image to each digit based on its shape. For example 0 can be an egg, 1 a pencil, 2 a swan, 3 lips, 4 a sail, 5 a unicycle, 6 a cherry, 7 a boomerang, 8 a snowman, and 9 a balloon on a string. The mental visual images can then be linked to produce longer numbers. More examples of additional numbers can be seen at Art of Memory: The Shaper System and Art of Memory: How to Use the Number Shape System.

The "Rhyme System" assigns a mental visual image to each digit by a word that rhymes with the digit. For example 0 is a hero, 1 is a gun, 2 is a shoe, 3 is a tree, 4 is a door, 5 is a hive, 6 is sticks, 7 is heaven, 8 is a gate and 9 is a vine or wine. The mental visual images can then be linked to produce longer numbers. More information can be found at Magnetic Memory Method.

It is readily apprarent these systems while simple are not as powerful as the Major System. SOmetimes if a limited number of numbers are needed thay are adequate and in the in the 2025 American Heart Association Basic Life Support Algorithms there are really not many different numbers needed.
