Spaced Repetition (practice)

Spaced repetition is a memory technique that involves reviewing previously learned material at increasing intervals of time. Of course just about all of us have done this in our studies using rote memorization.

Though not near as much as with rote memorization you still will need to practice your mental images and associations by spaced repetition. Practice involves repeating the mental images and associations until none are missed.

How often you practice will be based on your individual tendacies, the complexity of the material and how especially long you need to retain the memory.

If it is something just needing to be recalled short term such as a shopping list just the initial mental visual images and link associations are all that are needed. The shopping list can bascially be "rewritten" with new mental visual images the next week without the old images competing. This would be simular to using a black board and chalk.

If it is something important and needing to be retained long term practcing several times on the first day and then daily for several days and then maybe weekly several times and then just occasionally such as monthly would usually be adequate. This would be simular to itching in stone.

As discussed in Memory Systems learning new information, at least for me, is not near as dreadful with memory system techniques. I also find reviewing information previously learned using a memory system techniques is more enjoyable.
